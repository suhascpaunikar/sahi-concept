01 / Summary

Summary and the decision needed

PRD v1.4 puts the Quick Action Wheel on the timeframe pill of each chart (V1 below). On the index chart that pill sits in the top 15% of the screen, about 128 mm from the corner a right thumb pivots on. That is out of one-handed reach on a 6.5-inch phone. Its quarter fan also gives each action a 6.6 mm target, smaller than the 7 mm people need even at the centre of the screen. A long-press on the candles is already the crosshair, which you confirmed, so the wheel can't open by holding the chart itself.

This document compares six places to anchor the same seven-action wheel. The comparison uses a working prototype rebuilt from your four Sahi screenshots: the 412×915 Android screen matches within 1–3 px, and the candles were read pixel by pixel from your chart. Every number below comes from the prototype's own geometry on four phone sizes. No user-study results are claimed.

02 / Problem

What PRD v1.4 left open

  • Reach. v1.4 assumed the pill was the only anchor. The index chart's pill is at 14% of screen height. People who hold the phone in one hand (Hoober: 49% of observed use) have to regrip or use the other hand to reach it, and the fan then opens further up and left.
  • Fit. The 264–280 radius gate passes on every phone with the chart-only layout in your screenshots. It barely passes in an iPhone Safari tab (R 265–266) and fails with the order panel open (v1.4, C-01). A placement that needs less room would remove the fallback cases.
  • Target size. Seven slots in 90° give 11.9° each. At the label radius that is 6.6 mm across, below the 7 mm Hoober measured at the screen centre and well below the 9–12 mm he measured at edges and corners, where the pill and its fan sit.
  • Gesture budget. The candle area is taken: tap, drag to pan, pinch to zoom, and long-press for the crosshair (confirmed). The price axis scales on drag, the time axis zooms on drag, and the split handle resizes on drag. Any new anchor has to share space with one of these or add a control.
03 / Research

What the research says about placing a radial menu

#FindingWhat it means for Sahi
R1Pie menus beat linear menus: in Callahan, Hopkins, Weiser and Shneiderman (CHI 1988), users selected about 15% faster with fewer errors, because every item is the same distance away and larger. The cost is screen space.Keep the radial layout, but test a linear control (V6) on Sahi, because our slots are smaller than desktop pie slices.
R2Marking menus (Kurtenbach and Buxton): novices press and wait about ⅓ s for the menu, and experts flick in the remembered direction without waiting. In field use, marks were about 3.5× faster than selecting from the menu. Keeping errors under 10% allows 8 items per level at up to 2 levels.Seven items, one level and a 300 ms hold are in line. Directions must never move between sessions or placements, or experts can’t flick.
R3How people hold phones (Hoober 2013, 1,333 observations): 49% one hand, 36% cradled, 15% two hands.Optimise for one right thumb first, and mirror for left-handed use.
R4Touch accuracy (Hoober 2017): about 7 mm at the centre, 9 mm at the edges and 12 mm in the corners, and people prefer to touch the centre.Anchors and slots near edges and corners (V1, V2, V5) need larger targets than the 6.6 mm a quarter fan gives. Highlighting before release corrects some error, but the study has to measure it.
R5The thumb’s reachable area depends on device size, hand size and grip; the far top corner is the hardest (Bergstrom-Lehtovirta and Oulasvirta, CHI 2014).The index chart’s pill (V1, V6) needs a regrip on 6.1–6.7-inch phones. Anchors at mid-height or lower don’t.
R6Bezel menus started from the screen edge can be fast and eyes-free once learned (Jain and Balakrishnan, CHI 2012). But Android uses both side edges for Back, and lets an app exclude at most 200 dp of each edge and none of the bottom home-gesture area. Apple’s guidelines say not to interfere with system edge gestures.Rule out edge swipes. Any anchor within 24 dp of the right edge, where the slide goes left, will be read as Back on Android. That affects the pill’s right end, the axis corner, the price axis and the dock, which need exclusion rectangles or a hit area moved inwards.
R7Android’s default long-press timeout is 400 ms; AOSP lowered it from 500.A 300 ms hold opens our wheel before any system long-press. The crosshair keeps its own long-press on the candles.
R8Shipped hold-and-slide menus: Procreate’s QuickMenu (six buttons in a radial, touch and drag to select), Pinterest-style hold menus (an arc of actions around the finger, drag and release), iOS pull-down buttons opened by touch and hold (Safari’s Tabs button), Material 3’s FAB menu (2–6 actions that open where the button is), and ThumbMenu (an arc in the bottom-right corner for the thumb).Each placement here borrows a pattern traders have seen in other apps. The FAB guidance also caps a floating menu at six actions; we have seven.

Most of these figures come from the papers’ abstracts and from secondary summaries, not the full texts (the full PDFs weren’t reachable from this environment). Treat the numbers as directional, and check R1, R2 and R6 against the originals before quoting them. Sources are listed in Appendix B.

05 / Shared rules

Rules every placement shares

Only the anchor, shape and scope change between placements. Everything else is v1.4 behaviour, so the comparison isolates placement.

  • Hold to open. Hold still for 300 ms within 10 px to open the wheel. Moving first hands the touch to the region’s own gesture (scale the axis, resize the split) or cancels (pill, corner, dock). A tap keeps its current meaning: the pill opens the chart menu and the dock opens the Quick actions list.
  • Same seven actions, same order. 1m, 15m, Trend line, Fib retracement, VWAP, EMA 20, Clean view, with the configurable slots from v1.4 at their defaults. Order runs from the slot next to the horizontal outward. In V4 the two halves mirror each other; in V6 the list runs top to bottom.
  • Strict hit test. The inner zone, outside the ring, separators and the current timeframe all cancel. Nothing is clamped, so releasing anywhere that isn’t clearly a slot is a safe cancel.
  • Feedback. A light impact on open, a selection tick when the highlighted slot changes (at most once per 50 ms), a success pattern once the change has applied, and a soft tick on cancel. The centre of the wheel names the chart and what release will do.
  • Fallback. If the wheel doesn’t fit, the hold shows “Not enough room for the wheel · release for Quick actions”, and release opens the list.
  • Interruptions. One wheel at a time. A second finger, losing focus or a sheet opening cancels the gesture.
  • Accessibility. Every action is also in the Quick actions list, opened with a tap from the ⋮ menu, from the dock (V5) or from the fallback.

09 / Recommendation

Recommendation and decision rule

  1. Study shortlist: V1 (control), V4, V5 and V6 (linear control). Add V2 if there’s time, because it’s the fallback if V4’s resize conflict is too high.
  2. Drop V3. It is invisible, it shares its anchor with axis scaling, it collides with the Android back swipe, and it opens on only 35–54% of the option axis.
  3. If shipping before a study: V4 in split view plus V1 everywhere. V4 adds no chrome, makes the chart choice part of the gesture, has the largest targets and fits every phone. V1 keeps the familiar entry and covers single-chart layouts.
  4. Decision rule: pick the placement with the lowest median time to apply among those that pass every guardrail:
    • Accidental opens: no more than 2% of resize or scale attempts on a shared anchor (V4, V3).
    • Wrong-chart changes: no more than 3% of commits (V4, V5).
    • Wrong actions: no more than 5% of commits.
    • No drop in crosshair, resize or axis-scale use compared with V1.
    • Rated at least as comfortable as V1 for one-handed use.
10 / Validation

Study plan

  • Who: 12–16 Sahi traders who trade F&O on their phones. Half should use the phone one-handed for the session.
  • Design: within-subjects. Each person uses V1, V4, V5 and V6, with order counterbalanced by a Latin square. For each placement: the coach tip, one practice run of the prototype’s Timed tasks, then one measured run (8 tasks in random order).
  • Tasks: switch a chart to 1m or 15m, turn VWAP, EMA 20 or Clean view on, and start a trend line or Fib. Tasks are split across both charts and each starts from 5m with nothing on the chart.
  • Measures (logged by the prototype): time from task shown to commit; wrong actions; cancels; fallbacks; holds that interrupted a resize or scale; one-handed comfort (1–5); and a final ranking. Results are kept on the device and exported as JSON.
  • Devices: participants’ own phones, a mix of Android and iPhone, with the prototype added to the Home Screen so the browser bars don’t take height.
  • Then: run the winner against V1 in a beta build for two weeks, using the analytics in Section 12.

14 / Prototype

Using the prototype

  • Where: prototype/quick-wheel-placements/index.html, a single self-contained file. On a computer, a panel next to the phone switches placement, phone size and options and shows the event log. On a phone, the ⋮ button in the top bar opens the same choices.
  • Links: add ?v=1…?v=6 to open a placement, &frame=small|iphone|max for another phone size, &hand=left for left-handed, &zones=1 for the touch-zone overlay, &touches=1 to show touches, and &coach=0 to hide the first-run tip.
  • What works: all six placements with haptics, the timeframe menu, Settings (all seven chart types and the view toggles), Add indicators (EMA, SMA, VWAP, Bollinger Bands and Key Levels are drawn; search and tabs work), Range, drawing tools, crosshair, pan, pinch, axis scaling, split resizing, the Quick actions list, “Show me how” demos and Timed tasks.
  • What doesn’t: orders, symbol search, Sahi AI, landscape and live prices. The prices on screen are the ones in your screenshots.
  • Haptics: vibration on Android (tap the page once first); a light tick on iOS 18 Safari as a best effort; on-screen pulses and optional sound on a computer.
  • On a real Android phone: V3 and the right ends of V2 and V5 are close enough to the edge that a slide left can trigger Back (R6). That’s the conflict in PL-07, and it shows up in testing too: hold a finger’s width in from the edge.
